# Workout Program Rationale

## Scope and clinical boundary

The program balances three outcomes: full-body strength, at least 150 minutes of formal aerobic work each week, and a gated return to running, jumping, cutting, and basketball. JT's localized load-dependent pain below the left kneecap is treated as a **working hypothesis consistent with patellar tendon pain, not a diagnosis**. A sports PT or sports-medicine assessment is recommended and is required before cutting or contact basketball.

## Why the week is structured this way

Phases 1 and 2 use Monday, Wednesday, and Friday progressive quadriceps/tendon resistance plus full-body compounds, with low-impact aerobic work on Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday. In Phase 1 all formal conditioning is low impact because running, jumping, cutting, and repeated loaded stair climbing have been the symptom triggers or impose similar knee-extension demand. Phase 3 changes to a six-week high-low pattern: Monday A3 strength, Tuesday 45 minutes Zone 2, Wednesday a 30-minute stage-specific run, Thursday B3 strength plus 15 minutes easy cycling, Friday rest, and Sunday 60 minutes Zone 2. Week 1 Saturday is a second 1:2 run-walk exposure with no jumps. Weeks 2-6 Saturdays progress bilateral landings (3x5), pogos (3x15), jump rope (3x30 seconds) or low countermovement jumps (3x5), repeated submaximal jumps (3x5), then sport-specific jumps (3x4/side), each followed by 30 minutes low-impact Zone 2. Wednesday running progresses 1:2, 2:1, 3:1, 5:1, 10:1, then 30 minutes easy continuous. Phase 4 uses Monday A4, Tuesday Zone 2, Wednesday athletic work plus low-impact conditioning, Thursday B4 plus easy cycling, Friday Zone 2, a Saturday same-stage exposure, and Sunday Zone 2. This prevents three consecutive tendon-load days.

This avoids two bad extremes: complete rest that settles symptoms without restoring load capacity, and an overloaded mixed program that simultaneously adds running, StairMaster, jumping, cutting, and heavy quadriceps work.

## Tendon loading decisions

Progressive tendon-loading exercise has better support than an eccentric-only prescription, while contemporary clinical management organizes loading from symptom-modulating isometrics through isotonic/heavy slow resistance, energy storage, and sport-specific work. The program therefore uses three weekly resistance exposures, measured response rules, and manual gates rather than a calendar-only progression.

Spanish squats and leg-extension isometrics are optional pain tools, not daily hard work. Choose only one for 4x30-45 seconds with two minutes of rest immediately before scheduled resistance training, and only when it reduces pain; neither is prescribed between sessions. Goblet squats, dynamic split squats/RFESS, slow leg extensions, controlled step-downs, and single-leg box squats carry the progressive resistance work. Banded external rotation (2x12/side) and hip-flexor isometrics (2x20-30 seconds/side) are optional accessories; rotate at most two after the main work. Backward treadmill walking (5-8 minutes) and TKEs (2x15) are optional warm-ups. On Phase 2 Friday, a heel-elevated supported front-knee bend may be used after the main work at 2x6/side and a 3-0-2 tempo, but only in place of the single-leg box squat, never in addition. On Phase 3 Thursday, a reverse Nordic may be used after the main work at 2x6-8 and a 3-1-2 tempo, but only in place of the controlled step-down, never in addition. Step-down pulses are replaced with controlled repetitions; single-leg wall-sit and RFESS isometric variants are excluded as redundant. Resistance sets finish with 2-4 reps in reserve; add reps before load and change only one variable per week.

## Cardio and progression decisions

The World Health Organization recommends at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week for adults, alongside muscle strengthening. Every phase therefore contains at least 150 programmed aerobic minutes without counting incidental steps. Zone 2 means RPE 3-4/10 with the ability to speak in full sentences; a brisk walk counts only when it meets both. In Phase 1, Tuesday is freestyle/backstroke swimming or easy cycling; rowing is reserved for Thursday and only when tolerated. StairMaster requires seven stable days, pain-free normal stairs, and 3x8 controlled six-inch step-downs; it starts at 10 minutes replacing Zone 2 and adds five minutes after each stable week. Running requires seven stable days, a 60-minute brisk walk, 3x10 controlled step-downs, 10 single-leg box squats per side, and no next-day increase. It then begins with a 1:2 run-walk ratio. All-out sprinting is outside every phase.

The jump progression moves from bilateral landing to low pogos, jump rope or low countermovement jumps, repeated submaximal jumps, then sport-specific jumps. Every declared pre-cut run and jump stage is scheduled before Phase 4. Advancing to Phase 4 requires completing the exact Week 5 and Week 6 stages with stable responses, including two stable exposures at each applicable stage by repeating a week when needed, plus the existing step-down and clinician-assessment gates. Basketball moves from planned acceleration/deceleration to planned 45-degree cuts, planned 90-degree cuts, reactive cuts, non-contact practice, controlled contact practice, and games. Wednesday and Saturday exposure labels do not prove safe spacing: at least 72 elapsed hours must actually have passed. Otherwise impact is postponed and the card's low-impact fallback is used. Phase 3 Week 1 has no jumps until two stable 30-minute run exposures; an unstable Wednesday means Saturday is not counted as the second stable exposure and JT does not advance the pointer. Full games require two stable exposures at every stage, baseline symptoms the following morning, and clinician clearance.

## Response and stop rules

Pain during prescribed knee work may be up to 3/10 only when it does not escalate. Symptoms must be back to baseline the next morning. If worse, the next knee or impact dose drops 30-50%. A flare lasting more than 48 hours returns the program to the previous tolerated stage. Swelling, locking/catching, instability/giving way, inability to bear weight, night/rest pain, or worsening symptoms warrants clinical review. Chest pain, faintness, or breathlessness disproportionate to effort stops the session and warrants medical attention.
