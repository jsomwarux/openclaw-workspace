# 14-Day Protocol

**What this is:** a behavioral experiment with a defined endpoint, not a cure. It is designed to isolate variables you have never actually isolated. It runs alongside clinical appointments, not instead of them.

**What would make me wrong:** if you complete 14 days with genuine adherence to the four non-negotiables below and see no change in the daily score or the objective anchors, the psychophysiological model is weaker than I claimed and the next step is the sleep study plus a second opinion from a physiatrist or a specialist in functional neurological/somatic symptom disorders. That is a real possibility, not a formality.

---

## Two reversals of your prior approach

These matter more than any individual exercise.

**1. Stop stopping when you tense up.**

Your old rule was "stop any set where jaw clenching appears." Reverse it. Notice the clench, unclench, keep going. Stopping teaches your nervous system that muscle tension is a danger signal requiring immediate withdrawal — which is exactly the hypervigilance loop you're trying to exit. Graded exposure works by staying in the situation while the alarm fires and does not get confirmed.

The only true stop conditions are at the bottom of the exercise section, and none of them are "tension."

**2. Stop continuous self-monitoring.**

Rate symptoms **once per day, at a fixed time (9pm), and never at any other point.** No side-checking. No "is my exhale full right now." No mid-day activation scores. In a condition maintained by attention to bodily sensation, continuous tracking is an active ingredient in the problem. Your February–March logs are a record of that.

If you catch yourself scanning: name it ("scanning"), return attention to whatever you were doing. Do not analyze it, do not fix the tension, do not run a reset. This will feel wrong for about five days.

## The four non-negotiables

Everything else is optional. These four are the experiment.

| # | Rule | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Zero alcohol for all 14 days.** No exceptions, including social events. | This is the cleanest single variable available. Two weeks fully clears the rebound cycle. If a wedding or event falls in the window, go and drink nothing — that is also the exposure trial for socializing without it. |
| 2 | **Aerobic exercise 4 days/week.** Return to what worked: 30 min stairmaster or incline treadmill, moderate. Plus hot yoga 1×/week if you can get there. | You already have personal evidence this works. This is restoration, not a new program. |
| 3 | **In-person social contact 5 days/week.** Minimum 30 minutes. Coffee, gym, a walk with someone, a work meeting that isn't a call. | You explicitly reported this helps. During flares you removed it. Non-negotiable *especially* on bad days — that's the point. |
| 4 | **Phone/markets in one bounded window.** Crypto charts, Twitter, price checking: 30 minutes/day, at a fixed time, never before 10am and never after 8pm. Notifications off. | You identified this as the proximal trigger before the flare. This is not about crypto being bad — it's about intermittent variable reward with money attached being about the strongest sympathetic driver available on a phone. |

## Daily structure

**Morning (20 min)**
- Fixed wake time, 7:30am, all 14 days including weekends.
- Measure resting heart rate before getting out of bed (phone app or finger on wrist, 30 sec × 2). Write the number down. This is your objective anchor.
- One coffee, with food, before 10am. Keep it — removing it adds a withdrawal variable.
- Full glass of water.
- Do **not** do a body scan or a tension check.

**Workday**
- Set an hourly timer. When it fires, do exactly one thing: check whether your teeth are touching. If they are, part them, let the tongue rest on the roof of the mouth behind the front teeth, lips closed. Take one normal breath. Return to work. Total time: 5 seconds. This is habit-reversal training for awake bruxism and it is the highest-value thing you'll do all day.
- Stand and walk for 3 minutes every 60–90 minutes. Not stretching. Just moving.
- Raise the monitor so the top of the screen is at eye level, however crudely — books work. This is a 10-minute fix and worth doing even though it isn't the core issue.
- Lunch away from the desk.

**Pre-exercise (5 min)**
- Walk 5 minutes at an easy pace. That's the entire warm-up. No corrective work, no activation drills, no breathing protocol.

**Evening**
- Last meal by 8pm — reflux is a common co-driver of laryngeal tension and it's free to test.
- No screens showing charts after 8pm.
- 9pm: fill in the tracker. Once. Two minutes.

**Before bed (10 min)**
- Bed at 11pm.
- Ten minutes of extended-exhale breathing: in through the nose for 4, out for 8, sitting or lying. This is the *only* breathwork in the protocol. You've done years of this; concentrating it into one bedtime block reduces its role as a daytime monitoring ritual.
- If you wake in the night and can't get comfortable: get out of bed, sit in another room in low light, read something boring, return when sleepy. Do not lie there troubleshooting your shoulder.

## Exercise

**Weeks 1–2 — restoration only. No lifting yet.**

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Mon | Stairmaster or incline treadmill, 30 min, conversational pace |
| Tue | Walk 30 min outdoors |
| Wed | Stairmaster/incline, 30 min |
| Thu | Hot yoga (or walk 30 min) |
| Fri | Stairmaster/incline, 30 min |
| Sat | Walk 30 min outdoors |
| Sun | Stairmaster/incline, 30 min, or rest |

**Intensity rule:** you should be able to speak a full sentence throughout. If you can't, slow down. Your report that intense cardio makes things worse is likely accurate — high-intensity work in someone with high baseline arousal adds load. Moderate steady-state does not.

**The pain/tension rule — judge by tomorrow, not today.**

Tension *during* and *right after* a session is expected and is not a stop signal. What matters is the next morning.

- Next morning same or better → repeat or progress.
- Next morning worse but back to baseline within 24h → hold at that level, don't progress.
- Next morning worse for more than 48h → cut duration by a third for one week, then rebuild.

**Real stop conditions (stop the session, and if any occur, call your doctor):**
- Chest pain or pressure
- True shortness of breath disproportionate to effort
- Faintness, or vision going grey
- New weakness in a limb, or a limb giving way
- Sudden severe headache

Tension, tightness, clenching, asymmetry, and the sense of an incomplete breath are **not** on this list.

**Weight training:** deliberately excluded for 14 days. Not because lifting is bad for you — because you've attributed tension to it, and adding it now would confound the experiment. If day 14 goes well, reintroduce at week 3 with two full-body sessions per week at genuinely light loads, and progress slowly. When you get there, use the same "judge by tomorrow" rule, and do not build a program around correcting asymmetry.

## Food, hydration, caffeine, supplements

**Evidence-based**
- Zero alcohol (see above — this is the main one)
- Eat on a regular schedule; don't skip meals. <cite>Reactive hypoglycemia amplifies anxiety symptoms,</cite> and long gaps are a real and unnecessary arousal input.
- Hydration: your voice loss after the wedding had a large dehydration component, and vocal folds are unforgiving about it. Aim for pale-yellow urine.

**Low-risk, reasonable**
- Keep the single morning coffee, with food. Nothing after noon.
- Last meal 3 hours before bed (reflux/LPR trial).

**Clinician-dependent — don't guess**
- You previously took magnesium, D3, B12 and ashwagandha, saw nothing, and stopped. That was reasonable. Don't restart them blind. Get D, B12, ferritin and magnesium **measured** on the next blood draw and supplement only what's actually low.
- Ashwagandha in particular: don't restart while on sertraline without asking your prescriber.

## Daily tracker — under 2 minutes, 9pm only

Copy this into a notes app or a spreadsheet. Seven fields.

```
DATE:
Resting HR on waking:        ___ bpm
Hours slept (estimate):      ___
Alcohol today:               Y / N
Aerobic exercise today:      Y / N
In-person social ≥30 min:    Y / N
Awake clenching noticed:     0-5
Overall symptom burden:      0-10
One line — anything notable:
```

That's it. No side, no site, no exhale rating, no activation score, no tension map. Five of the seven fields are behaviors or objective numbers, deliberately. The point is to make the record about what you *did*, not about what you *felt*.

## What counts as improvement, plateau, or worsening

Judge on **7-day rolling averages**, never day to day. Day-to-day variation in this condition is noise and reading it as signal is part of the problem.

**Improvement**
- Resting HR trending down (even 3–5 bpm is meaningful)
- Symptom score average down ≥2 points week 2 vs week 1
- More days where you did the aerobic session and the social contact
- Fewer awake-clenching notices
- The subtler one, and the best predictor: bad days getting *shorter*, even if they're not less frequent

**Plateau**
- Averages flat across both weeks despite ≥80% adherence to the four non-negotiables
- Behaviors happening but symptom score unmoved
- → Not a failure. This means the behavioral layer isn't sufficient alone and the answer is upstream: medication review, sleep study, and the specialist referrals. Bring the tracker to those appointments — two weeks of clean data is genuinely useful to a clinician.

**Worsening — escalate, don't push through**
- Symptom average rising despite adherence
- Sleep getting worse
- Mood dropping, hopelessness deepening, or withdrawal increasing
- Any new neurological symptom
- → Call your prescriber. Do not wait for day 14. In particular, if mood worsens on sertraline, that is specifically something to report.

## Daily decision tree

**Every morning, one question: is today green, amber, or red?**

**GREEN — symptoms mild, functioning normally**
→ Run the full protocol. Don't skip the social contact because you feel fine and want to work.

**AMBER — noticeable tension, some anxiety, but functional**
→ Run the full protocol anyway. This is the important one. Amber days are where the avoidance habit forms. Reduce exercise intensity if you want, but do not skip the session and do not skip the social contact. If you're deciding whether to cancel plans: go.

**RED — debilitating, brain fog, can't face people**
→ Drop to the red-day minimum, which is still not zero:
1. Get outside and walk 20 minutes. Outdoors, not a treadmill.
2. One low-stakes human contact — a friend on the phone, your partner, a person at the gym counter. It does not have to be a real conversation.
3. Keep the fixed wake time and fixed bedtime.
4. Zero alcohol. This is the day the pull is strongest and the day it costs the most.
5. No troubleshooting your body. No new stretches, no research, no protocol design.
6. Fill in the tracker at 9pm and stop.

The red-day rule exists because your own data says isolation makes it worse and socializing makes it better. On red days your instinct will be to sit on the couch alone. That instinct is the illness, not a message from your body about what it needs.

**Same-day medical care, regardless of colour:**
- Chest pain or pressure; true breathlessness at rest
- New weakness, foot drop, dropping things
- Inability to urinate at all, or new incontinence
- Numbness in the groin or inner thighs
- Painful red eye or vision change
- Any thought of harming yourself → 988 (call or text), and tell someone that day

## Day 14

Don't extend on autopilot. Sit down with the tracker and answer three questions:

1. Did I actually hit the four non-negotiables, honestly, on how many days out of 14?
2. What did the resting HR do?
3. Did bad days get shorter?

Then bring the answers, and the tracker, to your appointments.
