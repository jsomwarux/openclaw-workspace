A delinquency report, lease date, and owner email should not give one workflow open access to every place the answer might live.

That is the part of agent governance that matters for real offices.

The workflow may need the delinquency report, lease dates, owner notes, prior emails, payment status, and a draft follow-up.

Some of that can be read.

Some of it can be prepared.

Some of it should stop until a named person approves the next step.

The control row I want before any sensitive action is pretty plain:

- represented owner
- workflow identity
- approved source
- approved tool path
- credential holder
- permission scope
- review rule
- final decision
- audit path

The credential holder matters.

If the agent is carrying the token, the office is trusting the agent.

If the gateway or toolbox holds the credential, the office can enforce the policy before the action moves.

I have been mapping this around property-management cash gates because the operational risk is obvious. A stale rent follow-up and a lease-renewal deposit chase are more than inbox tasks. They touch money, tenant history, owner approval, notice timing, and the final client record.

Routine prep should move faster.

Sensitive rows need an authority record.

That is the version of AI implementation I trust for established businesses: the workflow can help the office, but the credential boundary, approval rule, and audit trail decide what actually moves.
