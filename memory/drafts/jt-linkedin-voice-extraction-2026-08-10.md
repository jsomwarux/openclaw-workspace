# JT LinkedIn Voice Extraction

## 1. SENTENCE MECHANICS

Typical sentence length is short-to-medium. Most public lines should be 7-18 words.

Proof posts can use longer explanatory sentences, usually 18-35 words, when the sentence is carrying a constraint stack: source data, system of record, approval owner, exception path, metric, or deployment detail.

Use short sentences for verdicts, transitions, and proof points:

- Some rows are routine.
- Some rows touch money, tenant communication, owner judgment, or legal timing.
- The workflow needs to know the difference before it drafts anything.
- Facts. But distribution is the new moat.
- Send it.

Fragments are allowed when they are conversationally earned or when a metric/proof point is stronger without explanation. One-word or two-word fragments are rare on LinkedIn and more natural on X or replies. Do not turn LinkedIn into dramatic fragment stacks.

Second clauses are common. The natural pattern is a plain first claim plus a practical reason, caveat, dependency, or consequence.

Common connectors: but, because, before, when, if, so, and without.

The second clause should add operational clarity, not polish. Bad second clauses sound like generic thought leadership. Good second clauses name the dependency or risk.

## 2. OPENINGS

JT's posts usually start with the work, the scene, the constraint, or the proof. They do not start with throat clearing.

Real opening lines or opening candidates from published/logged drafts:

1. Built this AI workflow for Yair and his real estate firm.
2. Yesterday I installed a dedicated automation PC for a NYC real estate family office.
3. This is essentially what I attempted to build with http://adversightai.com almost a year ago.
4. Context on how this works: GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Pro, Opus 4.5, and Grok 4 work together and cross-check other to provide optimal analysis with a focus on game theory positioning.
5. A delinquency balance, a lease date, and a deposit chase should not move through the same AI workflow without a decision trail.

Another real draft opener from the corpus:

- Lead capturing from NYC ballet family contributors page

Opening patterns JT does not use:

- Great question.
- Here is what I have been thinking about.
- I have been thinking a lot about AI.
- Most AI projects do not fail because X. They fail because Y.
- Most SMBs do not need X. They need Y.
- The blocker is not X, it is Y.
- What most people miss...
- Here is the system:
- Here is what you need to do:
- The results?
- Would you trust an AI agent with your bank account?
- The useful question is uglier...
- No strategy decks. No jargon. Just results.
- No meetings. No overhead. Just automation.
- Insult or hype hooks like "you're a moron," "INSANE opportunity," "DO NOT," "EVERYTHING," or "PISSED."

## 3. VOCABULARY

Repeated natural words and phrases:

workflow, handoff, owner, approval, source, queue, system, exception, intake, record, check, proof, deployment, local, messy, concrete, route, logs, source record, system of record, approval boundary, review queue, final outcome, decision trail, operating path, existing systems, sensitive rows, safe operating environment.

Industry terms JT uses naturally:

- Property operations: delinquency balance, lease date, deposit chase, tenant communication, owner judgment, legal timing, AppFolio, COI, rent delinquency, lease renewal.
- Workflow implementation: source report, approval owner, exception path, audit trail, human review, confidence threshold, final record.
- SMB AI: AI workflow, AI implementation, automated workflows, local deployment, dedicated mini PC, existing files, review item, source attached.
- Salesforce and enterprise-adjacent: Agentforce, Data Cloud, records, owners, approval workflows.
- Crypto and technical analysis: model stack, cross-check, risk/return, game theory positioning.

Use these as operating objects, not buzzwords.

Words to avoid completely in public content:

utilize, synergy, ecosystem, scalable, streamline, actionable, bandwidth, holistic, innovative, robust, cutting-edge, best-in-class, thought leader, value-add, paradigm, granular, deliverable, upskill, touchpoint, disruptive, transformative, implementation excellence, unlock, unpack, proactive, learnings.

Phrases rejected or edited out:

- AI is changing everything.
- The tools exist. The implementation does not.
- Agents handle the work. You keep the margin.
- Demo proves it is possible. Deploy proves it is real.
- The implementation is always the bottleneck.
- It matters more than people think.
- That is not automation. That is leverage.

- AI chatbot contrast framing.

## 4. PUNCTUATION AND FORMATTING

No em dashes.

Do not use exclamation points in normal LinkedIn posts.

No emoji by default. Use emoji only if explicitly requested or if the detail is a real human/contextual object, not decoration.

No hashtags by default. If hashtags are explicitly requested, use three relevant hashtags at the end, not inside the post.

LinkedIn formatting uses 3-6 short paragraphs with blank lines between them.

One idea per paragraph.

Bullets are allowed when the list is functional: inputs, checks, workflow steps, owners, approval paths, or constraints. Do not use bullets to create fake momentum.

Use normal sentence case.

Do not use all-caps emphasis.

Do not use colon-hook machinery as the default opening shape.

Do not use tricolon negation patterns like "No X. No Y. Just Z."

## 5. STANCE

Unsupported claims get removed, narrowed, or marked as projected.

Verified numbers can be stated bluntly. Projected numbers must be labeled as projected, estimated, forecast-only, or based on assumptions.

Do not imply a company is a client unless it is true.

Do not imply a payment, acceptance, deployment, or production outcome happened unless it is verified.

JT does not hedge by softening every sentence with "I think," "in my opinion," or "from my perspective."

He hedges with practical qualifiers when uncertainty is real: probably, pretty, I am unsure, if, should, or would.

Predictions are rare and should be tied to an operating reason. JT is more natural naming what a workflow needs than making broad future claims.

When talking about his own work, lead with ownership only when proof exists: Built this, Installed, I put together, I would start with. Then name the architecture, constraint, or metric. Do not humblebrag.

When talking about other people's work, translate the public signal into an operational problem. Do not dunk on them. Do not make JT's value depend on saying others are bad.

Questions to the reader are allowed but not required. LinkedIn can end with a clean insight or a direct question. Never end with engagement bait like "what do you think," "DM me," "link in bio," or "follow me for more."

## 6. STRUCTURE

Template 1: verified build proof

Beat 1: Ownership line.
Beat 2: Deployment surface or operating detail.
Beat 3: Workflow scope or examples.
Beat 4: Safety or approval rationale.
Beat 5: Human exception boundary.
Beat 6: Metric or projected outcome.
Beat 7: Vertical thesis or practical implication.

Example shape:

Built this for X.

Dedicated machine / existing files / local setup.

It handles A, B, C.

Sensitive rows stay in review.

Metric.

This is what real implementation means for established businesses.

Template 2: buyer-scene consulting post

Beat 1: Concrete workday scene.
Beat 2: Specific examples of inputs or handoffs.
Beat 3: Plain diagnosis.
Beat 4: Where AI fits.
Beat 5: Owner / approval / exception path.
Beat 6: Cleaner operating outcome.

Example shape:

A tenant email, vendor note, lease date, and owner rule should not become separate follow-up systems.

The workflow reads the source, checks the record, drafts the clean path, and holds the sensitive case for approval.

That is the useful version of AI implementation for a real office.

Template 3: AI Ops teardown

Beat 1: Public signal or observed workflow.
Beat 2: The operating problem underneath it.
Beat 3: The system boundary.
Beat 4: The practical build path.
Beat 5: The risk/approval constraint.
Beat 6: The clean takeaway.

## 7. WHAT I HAVE EDITED OUT

Generic AI consultant posture gets removed. JT sounds worse when copy tries to position him as a generic AI strategist instead of an operator who can see the messy handoffs.

Framework-announcing hooks get removed. "Here is the framework" usually sounds less like JT than simply naming the work and the constraint.

Contrarian reveal patterns get removed or rewritten. This includes "not X, Y," "most people think X, but actually Y," and "the blocker is not X."

Colon hooks get reduced. JT can use a colon in a technical/context line, but not as the default creator-hook rhythm.

Outreach/process meta gets cut unless it proves a buyer problem. Internal content-system transparency is not automatically post-worthy.

Casual builder diary language gets tightened on LinkedIn. "Played around with this," "messing with," and "quick build" can undercut the work if the topic involves real client operations.

Fake proof gets removed. Do not say shipped, deployed, accepted, saved money, replaced work, or generated revenue unless the proof exists.

Polished aphorisms get removed. Lines like "That is not automation. That is leverage." are too neat and sound generated.

Excessive X compression gets expanded on LinkedIn. LinkedIn needs enough operational context that a buyer can see the system.

Corporate smoothing gets removed. Avoid "in today's landscape," "broader implications," "centralized source of truth," and "streamline execution."

Trust-question hooks get removed. "Would you trust an AI agent with your bank account?" is not JT's lane.

Dramatic one-word noun stacks get removed.

Repeated "exception layer" phrasing gets varied or replaced with named approval paths, review queues, source records, or owners.

Build-list flex CTAs get removed. JT should not close posts by flexing a list of things he can build.
