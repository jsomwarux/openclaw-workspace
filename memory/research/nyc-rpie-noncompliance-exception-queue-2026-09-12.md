# NYC RPIE noncompliance exception queue — 2026-09-12

Internal client/market research for JT's property-operations consulting lane. Do not send, post, schedule, or create outreach from this note without JT approval.

## Verified signal

- NYC Department of Finance has published borough-level lists of properties that did not file a 2025 Real Property Income and Expense statement by the June 1, 2026 deadline.
- Published penalties scale with assessed value from $300 to $100,000. Required exclusion filers also face penalties, and three consecutive years of nonfiling can expose an income-producing property to a penalty equal to five percent of final actual assessed value.
- This is a post-deadline remediation problem with a public exception population, not a generic future-compliance reminder.

Primary source: [NYC Department of Finance — RPIE non-compliance penalties](https://www.nyc.gov/site/finance/property/property-rpie-non-compliance.page)

## Buyer/workflow implication

For a property manager, owner representative, or family office with multiple NYC assets, the useful control layer is a portfolio exception queue:

1. reconcile each BBL against the official nonfiler list;
2. assign owner, accountant, managing agent, or counsel responsibility;
3. record whether the remedy is a filing, exclusion claim, or status challenge;
4. attach submission/response evidence and keep unresolved items visible;
5. escalate repeat-year exposure separately from first-year exceptions.

This supports JT's existing positioning: **source-backed exception detection, named ownership, evidence capture, and human-controlled remediation**. It is strongest as a diagnostic question in a warm property-operator conversation, not as a new product or unsolicited compliance claim.
